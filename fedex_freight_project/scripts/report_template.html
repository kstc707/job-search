FedEx Freight, from public data

Market or share?

FedEx Freight has lost a sixth of its share of the five largest public LTL carriers' shipments since mid-2021. A model estimated only on its four competitors says how much of that is price, and how much is something price cannot explain. The forecasts were fixed and fingerprinted before any of these carriers report.

Q1

How much of the share loss is price?

Across Old Dominion, Saia, XPO and ArcBest, a carrier whose weight-adjusted price rises 1% faster than its competitors' loses about {{elas_b0}}% of its shipment share over the same year (95% CI {{elas_ci}} for the coefficient), and about {{elas_lr}}% once the following quarter is included. The relationship survives a placebo test that shuffles prices within each carrier (p = {{placebo_p}}), switching to prices excluding fuel, and adding quarter fixed effects. FedEx is left out of the estimation, so applying it to FedEx is an out-of-sample test.

The answer changes over time. In the year to mid-2023 FedEx raised its relative price about {{rel_2023}}%, and price explains essentially all of that year's share loss: the yield-first strategy cost share. In the year after Yellow shut down, and again in the year to mid-2026, FedEx lost share while its relative price was falling. Those losses, {{np_2024}} and {{np_2026}} log-points, are not price. The second one lines up with the spin-off and the unwinding of bundled parcel pricing.

Q2

Forecasts made before the answers exist

The models, metrics and success criteria were written down and committed before any model was fitted. Each model was then refit at {{n_dates}} past issue dates on data published before that date, with feature availability gated by the EDGAR filing date of every monthly update, and scored on {{n_test}} carrier-quarters from 2022 to 2026.

The honest result: a carrier's own monthly updates inside the quarter cut error {{own_gain}}% against carrying last quarter forward (Diebold-Mariano p = {{own_p}}). Ridge, gradient boosting and their ensemble improved {{ml_gain}}%, which is not significant across 36 dates (p = {{ens_p}}). Competitors' data alone made it worse. For FedEx, which publishes no monthly updates, nothing beat the naive forecast. With FedEx's own daily shipment data, a quarter-end nowcast is close to solved; the open problems are longer horizons and account-level share shifts.

Next

What I would build with FedEx Freight's data

Retention

Account-level share early warning

The non-price share loss is an account problem. Model each account's expected weekly shipments, flag drift before it becomes churn, and rank accounts for the sales team by expected volume at risk, starting with the roughly 10% that were bundled with parcel.

Validated the same way as here: pre-registered, rolling-origin, scored against a naive baseline.

Pricing

Price response by segment

The share elasticity here is an industry average. Estimate it by lane, customer size and commodity from renewals and general rate increases, and use the {{ratio}}-to-1 price-volume exchange rate as the guardrail.

Outcome: rate actions that clear break-even; accounts where a smaller increase keeps more profit.

Revenue integrity

Density and dimensioner audit

With dimensioners on more than 90% of shipments and density-based freight classes, predict each shipment's density and send the largest expected differences to inspection first.

Outcome: reclass revenue per inspection against today's hit rate.

Fuel

Fuel surcharge exposure

Break the {{rho}} realized-to-tariff ratio down by contract to find caps and discounts before diesel falls and fuel becomes a {{fuel27_abs}}% headwind.

Outcome: ex-fuel yield reporting and contract terms that hold when diesel falls.

QA

How it was tested

  • Pre-registration (targets, information sets, splits, metrics, success criteria) committed before any model was fitted. Every later change is logged with its reason in ds/DEVIATIONS.md.
  • Point-in-time features: a monthly update is used only if its EDGAR filing date is on or before the forecast date. Automated tests fail if any training window includes an unpublished target.
  • Significance: Diebold-Mariano with the Harvey-Leybourne-Newbold correction. Uncertainty: block-bootstrap confidence intervals and conformal prediction intervals with measured coverage.
  • Share model: estimated on competitors only, applied to FedEx out of sample; permutation placebo; leave-one-carrier-out prediction; four robustness specifications.
  • Negative results are reported: ML did not beat persistence significantly; nothing beat naive for FedEx alone; changepoint detection found no reliable break (its simulated false-alarm rate at this length is 24%).
  • Data tie-outs: 37 FedEx quarters reconcile to the 10-K; 1,139 peer filings reconcile to the carriers' own totals. {{n_tests}} automated tests pass.
