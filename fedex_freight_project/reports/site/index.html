FedEx Freight, from public data

Market or share?

FedEx Freight's daily shipments are down a quarter from their 2021 peak. This page separates what the freight market did from what is specific to FedEx, prices the trade-off between yield and volume, and puts a number on Jun to Sep 2026 before the company reports it.

Q1

Is the volume decline the market, or FedEx?

Between 2019 and 2024 the packaged and finished goods that move less-than-truckload fell 4.4% by weight, according to the federal Freight Analysis Framework. FedEx Freight's own tonnage fell 31.4%. Lighter shipments explain part of that: weight per shipment dropped from about 1,200 lbs to about 920. Most of the gap is shipments FedEx no longer carries.

The four public LTL carriers (Old Dominion, Saia, XPO and ArcBest) give a same-quarter benchmark. Since FY2023, FedEx Freight's shipments per day have run on average 5.6 points a year behind the peer index. The deepest gap, in FY2024, includes the year after Yellow shut down (July 2023), when Saia and XPO absorbed much of its freight; FedEx took less. The gap briefly turned positive in mid-2025, then reopened in the two quarters before the spin-off. Over the last four quarters it was +1.6, -0.5, -4.0, -5.7 points. The peer index itself was flat (-0.1%) over June to August 2026, while XPO grew shipments about 6% in August.

Price does not explain it. Adjusted for weight, FedEx's base price per hundredweight rose +2.1% to +2.7% in the quarter ended May 2026. Old Dominion's rose +6.3% and XPO's +3.3% in the calendar quarter to June, yet XPO gained volume. The share loss points to non-price causes: sales coverage through the transition, the unwinding of bundled parcel pricing, and service. Those are questions for account-level data.

Q2

What will FedEx report on October 29?

FDXF's first standalone release covers four months, June to September 2026. Every model below was re-estimated at each of 19 past quarters using only data available before that quarter, then scored on what actually happened. COVID base-effect quarters are excluded from training and scoring.

Two findings are negative, and both matter. Macro indicators (manufacturing output, truck tonnage, Cass shipments) did worse than simply carrying last quarter forward: mean absolute error 4.1 points against 3.8. Same-month peer volumes did not beat persistence either (4.4 points). FedEx's volume has been following its own trajectory, which is what a share problem looks like. The headline nowcast is a combination fixed in advance of one persistence, one peer and one macro model, so its backtest is not flattered by picking the winner afterwards. It scored 3.2 points, the best of the set.

Nowcast for Jun to Sep 2026: shipments per day -5.9% YoY, about 84.7k a day against roughly 89.9k a year earlier. 80% range -10.1% to -0.4%, taken from the model's own backtest errors. For comparison, BofA published −3.0% for the September quarter, and management said in September that volumes would exit 2026 flat to slightly positive.

Q3

Is pricing holding, or is it fuel?

FedEx Freight publishes its fuel surcharge tariff. In the eight quarters where it also disclosed revenue excluding fuel, it collected 0.59 times the tariff rate, with a standard deviation of 0.009. That steady ratio lets the tariff extrapolate to diesel prices the history never saw.

Tested on the quarter ended May 2026, when diesel spiked, the model puts base revenue per hundredweight at +0.3% YoY. Management described it as a slight decline. Weight per shipment rose 3%, and heavier shipments lower revenue per hundredweight mechanically. Estimated from Priority versus Economy freight with quarter fixed effects, a 10% heavier shipment carries about 7.7% lower revenue per hundredweight (elasticity -0.77, 95% CI -0.89 to -0.65). A naive time-series fit gives -1.35, which would mean heavier shipments earn less per shipment; it is biased because rate increases happened while weights fell. Across the four peers, with quarter and carrier fixed effects, the elasticity is -0.57 (95% CI -0.87 to -0.26), consistent with FedEx's own. Adjusted for weight, FedEx's like-for-like price was up +2.1% to +2.7% that quarter. Weight per shipment is rising across the industry as truckload tightens, so FedEx's 3% increase is not a FedEx-specific density effect.

Q4

What is a point of volume worth?

Over 27 quarters (FY2018 to FY2026, excluding COVID), each dollar of revenue gained from more shipments added about 24 cents of operating income (95% CI 15 to 32). Each dollar from price and mix added about 51 cents (42 to 61). One point of shipments is worth about $21M a year and one point of price about $45M, so a price increase pays for itself until it costs about 2.1 points of volume.

At the market's current valuation of about 19 times adjusted operating income, one point of shipments is worth roughly $2.66 a share. That is the scale of the question in Q1.

Q5

Where is the freight going?

FAF5's mid-range forecast has interstate LTL-type freight on lanes over 250 miles growing 2.9% a year to 2035. Georgia to Florida, Illinois to Michigan, and lanes into Texas add the most tons. From 2019 to 2024 the national total fell 4.4%; the Southeast, Texas and New Jersey outperformed what their commodity mix predicted, and the upper Midwest and Northeast lagged.

Freight is also unbalanced by direction. Tennessee receives more than it ships (-9.5% net), and Nevada, New Mexico and the Mountain states are heavily inbound. Those lanes are where backhaul pricing and network design pay.

Next

What I would build with FedEx Freight's data

Public data stops at the quarter and the company. Each of these takes the method above down to the account, lane or shipment, where the decisions are made.

Retention

Account-level volume early warning

Model each account's expected weekly shipments and flag drift before it shows up as churn, including the roughly 10% of accounts that were bundled with FedEx parcel. Rank the list for the 500-person sales team by expected volume at risk.

Outcome: points of shipments retained. Each point is worth about $21M of operating income a year.

Pricing

Price response by segment

Estimate how volume responds to rate changes by lane, customer size and commodity, using renewals and general rate increases as natural experiments. Use the 2.1-to-1 price-volume exchange rate as the guardrail in the new pricing system.

Outcome: rate actions that clear the break-even, accounts where a smaller increase keeps more profit.

Revenue integrity

Density and dimensioner audit

With dimensioners on more than 90% of shipments and freight classes now set by density, predict each shipment's density from shipper, commodity and history, and send the largest expected differences to inspection first.

Outcome: reclass revenue per inspection, measured against today's inspection hit rate.

Fuel

Fuel surcharge exposure

FedEx collects 0.59 times its published surcharge on average. Break that ratio down by contract to find fuel caps and discounts, and plan for 2027, when EIA's diesel forecast turns fuel from a +8.1% tailwind into a -7.7% headwind on revenue per shipment.

Outcome: ex-fuel yield reporting and contract terms that hold up when diesel falls.

Network

Imbalance-aware lane pricing

Combine lane-level directional balance with cube data from the dimensioners to price backhaul capacity into inbound-heavy markets and protect head-haul margins out of net-shipper states.

Outcome: fewer empty linehaul miles; higher revenue per trailer on balanced lanes.

Planning

Weekly volume nowcast

Run this page's backtested nowcast weekly with internal daily shipments and peer monthly updates, for capacity planning and investor guidance.

Outcome: forecast error tracked every quarter against a naive baseline, so the model has to earn its place.

QA

How the numbers were checked

  • Every download was checked to be real data rather than an error page. 37 quarters of FedEx Freight statistics were parsed from 47 FedEx Corp earnings releases.
  • FY2026 quarters add up to the 10-K: revenue $8,795M, segment operating income $616M, 86.1k shipments per day. Calendar-year tonnage reproduces FDXF's reported totals within 0.5%.
  • The backtest refits every model at each origin, and an automated test fails if any training window overlaps its test quarter.
  • The fuel model was tested on a quarter outside its diesel range and agrees with management's own description.
  • Peer statistics from 1139 SEC filings were tied out against the carriers' own totals (shipments per day × workdays, annual sums). The weight elasticity was estimated two independent ways: within FedEx, and across the four peers.
  • The LTL market proxy was rebuilt under stricter and broader commodity definitions; the market change stays within a point.
  • Known limits: about 37 quarterly observations; FAF 2018 to 2024 values are modeled; public peers are a sample of the LTL market, not all of it; nothing here uses FedEx internal data.
Public sources: SEC EDGAR (FedEx Corp CIK 1048911, FDXF CIK 2082247, ODFL, SAIA, XPO, ARCB), FRED (ATA tonnage, Cass, industrial production, PPI, EIA diesel), BTS/ORNL FAF5.7.1, fedexfreight.com fuel surcharge table, and press coverage of analyst estimates.
