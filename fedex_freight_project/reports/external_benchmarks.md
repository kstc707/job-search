## 1. FedEx Freight (FDXF): guidance, events and management commentary

### Transition-period guidance (7 months, June 1 to Dec 31, 2026)

This guidance comes from the Q4 FY2026 release and call on 2026-06-25:
[SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2082247/000162828026045515/fdxf-q4fy2026juneearningsr.htm)
(also in this repo) and the
[call transcript (Investing.com) [secondary]](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-fedex-freight-posts-solid-q4-2026-results-as-spinoff-costs-weigh-93CH-4761870).

**Headline guidance:**
- **Revenue:** +4% to +6% versus the same seven months of 2025. About 60% falls in the four months ending Sept 30.
- **Adjusted operating income:** **$605M to $645M**, with GAAP of $475–515M after about $130M of spin-off costs. The
  adjusted margin is 11.5–12.0%, and about 75% of the income falls in the four months ending Sept 30.
- **EPS:** adjusted diluted EPS is $2.40–2.60.

**Margin bridge given by CFO Marshall Witt on the call [secondary transcript]:**

| Driver | Effect on margin |
|---|---|
| Yield | +200 bp, "driven largely by fuel" |
| Network efficiencies | +80 bp |
| Performance-based compensation | −130 bp |
| TSA costs (about $65M) | −120 bp |
| Volume | −30 bp |

**Other call comments:**
- **Transition costs** were described as "a little north of" $600M over 12 months, and possibly $700–750M over 18
  months.
- **Seasonality:** management called calendar Q4 the softest quarter, with revenue down 5–7% and margin down about
  600 bp sequentially.
- **Customers** said there was "no significant breakage" in renewals after unbundling.
- **Pricing system:** the new system went live in May.
- **Future disclosure:** management said it will report revenue per hundredweight and per shipment, with and
  without fuel.

Sources: [FreightWaves [secondary]](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/fedex-freight-sets-goalposts-for-standalone-business)
and [MarketBeat/Yahoo [secondary]](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/fedex-freight-investor-day-standalone-020601332.html).

**Financial targets:**
- **Baseline:** about $8.7B of FY26 revenue and about $1.1B of adjusted operating income, excluding about $500M of

- **Margin:** adjusted margin goes from about **12% to about 15%**. No target year was given. Yield is expected to
  deliver more than half of the improvement.
- **Cash and capital:**
  - Capex about **5% of revenue**: 45% equipment, 25% facilities, 25% technology.
  - Free cash flow **over $1B a year**, with conversion above 90%.
  - Debt of $4.3B at the spin, with leverage **under 2.5x within 12 months**.

**Customers and sales:**
- About **140,000 customers**. The top 25 are **17% of revenue**, and customers of 10+ years are about 90% of

- Revenue mix is **64% Priority / 32% Economy / about 3% Custom Critical**.
- Bundled-pricing agreements are **99% unwound**, with existing contracts honored to the end of their terms.
- The dedicated LTL sales force has **500+ sellers**, focused on small and midsize businesses.

**Operations and technology (useful for the data-science pitch):**
- "Dim and Motion" dimensioning captures **more than 90% of shipments** in real time, which enables cube-based

- RFID is on about 90% of shipments.
- Cube utilization rose 12% year over year.
- Spare network capacity is about 30%.
- Planned versus actual freight handles fell 10%.
- Manual billing and invoice touches are expected to fall 60%.

## 4. NMFC density-based classification (Docket 2025-1)

- **Dates** ([NMFTA news](https://nmfta.org/news/nmfta-implements-historic-nmfc-changes-launches-classit-to-support-industry-in-adapting-to-new-classification-standards/),
  [ClassIT FAQ](https://nmfta.org/wp-content/media/2025/01/ClassITFAQ_1.6.2025.pdf)):
  - Docket 2025-1 took effect on **July 19, 2025**.
  - The ClassIT+ tool was released July 15, 2025.
- **What changed** ([NMFTA FAQ](https://help.nmfta.org/hc/en-us/articles/38932132157979-NMFC-Changes-FAQ-Can-you-share-with-us-the-density-and-class-breaks-for-the-new-13-sub-structure)):
  - The density scale expanded from **11 to 13 subs**: 30–<35 pcf is class 60, 35–<50 pcf is class 55, and 50+ pcf
    is class 50.
  - Items with special handling, stowability or liability concerns keep their own item classes.
  - Secondary sources say about 30% of classifications and 2,000+ items moved to pure density.
- **Enforcement:**
  - **[unverified]** One source says FedEx Freight delayed full enforcement to **Dec 1, 2025**
    ([ParcelPath [secondary]](https://parcelpath.com/freight-ltl/freight-freight-class-and-documentation/ltl-class/)).
  - Others say enforcement was live industry-wide by January 2026.
- **Revenue impact** (vendor blogs only, [GoShip](https://www.goship.com/blog/the-end-of-freight-class-how-density-based-pricing-is-quietly-changing-ltl-in-2026/) /
  [Packizon](https://packizon.com/nmfc-2026-changes-freight-costs-dimensioning/) [secondary]):
  - The impact on a typical shipper is **about ±2–3%**.
  - A one-class move (for example 100 → 125) is a **20–35%** premium on that shipment.
  - Reclass fees are typically $20–50 plus re-rating.
  - Shippers' systems often can't capture dimensions; see the
    [ACD comment letter](https://nmfta.org/wp-content/media/2025/02/ACD-Comments-NMFTA-Docket-2025-1.pdf).
- **Gap:** I found no carrier-reported reweigh or reclass revenue, and no FedEx-specific estimate. FedEx's
  dimensioning covers **more than 90%** of shipments (Investor Day).
- **Inference:** shipment-level density accuracy is a real revenue lever FedEx is equipped to measure. That fits
  "accurate weight/dimensions drive revenue".

## 5. Diesel spike, 2026

Source: [EIA October STEO](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_full.pdf), plus
[Forbes 2026-09-09](https://www.forbes.com/sites/rrapier/2026/09/09/why-diesel-is-on-track-to-set-a-2026-price-record/) and
[BOE Report 2026-10-22](https://boereport.com/2026/10/22/us-refiners-have-run-harder-in-2026-as-tight-diesel-supplies-boost-margins-eia-says/) [secondary].

**Prices:**
- Retail diesel **averaged $6.29/gal in September 2026**. EIA's weekly series hit $5.967 on Sept 7, above the June
  2022 record of $5.810.
- **This matches our FRED GASDESW data ($6.29 for September).**
- EIA forecasts **$5.19/gal for 2026** and **$4.49 for 2027**.

**Causes:**
- Higher crude prices and wider diesel crack spreads.
- Distillate stocks about **13% below normal**, plus strong export pull.
- Press attributes the spike mainly to the **2026 Iran war and Strait of Hormuz disruption**, with Ukrainian strikes
  on Russian refineries as a secondary factor.

**Effect on LTL:**
- Fuel surcharges took about 37% of base linehaul in May (FreightWaves, above).
- FDXF expects fuel to add about **200 bp** to transition-period margin.
- **Inference:** EIA's 2027 forecast implies a fuel-surcharge **headwind to revenue/shipment in 2027**. Yield
  analysis must strip out fuel.

## How this validates or challenges our analysis

**Facts found that confirm in-repo numbers:**
1. **Diesel at $6.29/gal in September 2026** exactly matches FRED GASDESW. EIA explains it as a supply shock (crude
   plus crack spreads), not demand.
2. **Fuel-driven yield.** Revenue/shipment +11.5% "driven by fuel" is confirmed by management ("substantially flat"
   ex-fuel) and by the industry picture (FreightWaves: headline +13.5%, base rates flat). Our decision to use ex-fuel
   metrics is right.
3. **Cass YoY negative for about 3.5 years** matches our "48 of 57 months negative since 2022". Cass now confirms the
   streak ended in August and September 2026 (+2.1%, +1.9%), consistent with our latest print (+1.9%).
4. **ADS −5.9% in FY26 Q4 and the spin-off margin squeeze** are confirmed by management. Investor Day's $1.1B
   adjusted operating income baseline plus about $500M of spin costs reconciles with our $616M segment operating
   income plus $492M separation costs ≈ $1.1B.
5. **LTL kept pricing discipline while truckload fell:** Yellow's exit took out about 10% of capacity, GRIs ran
   4.9–7.1%, and renewals were mid-to-high single digits. This fits our LTL PPI +23.6% versus truckload +7.9%.

**Challenges and new evidence (inference):**
1. **"Nothing unique about our volumes" (CFO, March 2026) is now hard to sustain.** In Jul–Aug 2026, XPO shipments
   per day were **+5.7–5.9%** and Saia's **+1%**, while ODFL was −2.4% and ArcBest about −3%. FedEx was guided to a
   mid-single-digit decline, with BofA at −3% for Q3. Cass and ATA are flat to positive. **The peer spread
   (FedEx about 3–9 points below XPO) is the strongest public evidence for a FedEx-specific share gap.** That is the
   question behind Option A.
2. **Bundling churn may be small directly** (about 10% of the base, repriced at a 1–2% discount, "minimal
   breakage"). The share gap may instead come from **yield-first pricing** and the sales-org transition. Note the
   CCO's exit on Sept 2. This sharpens the hypothesis to test.
3. **Weight per shipment is rising across the industry** (Saia +7%, ArcBest +8%, BofA's FedEx estimate +3%). The
   driver is tight truckload markets pushing heavy freight into LTL, so revenue/cwt comparisons need weight
   adjustment.
4. **Valuation:** FDXF is down about 27% since early June and now trades below most peers on forward P/E. The market
   is pricing in volume and disclosure risk. A **dated nowcast published before Oct 29** is timely, because the
   first standalone results arrive that day.
5. **Reconciliation flag:** the Oct 29 release covers **June to September (four months)**, not a calendar quarter.
   A nowcast should target that four-month window, or July–September with June shown separately.
